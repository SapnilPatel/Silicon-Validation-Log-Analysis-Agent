### 000015:THERMAL_TRIP
- **Root cause:** The most likely cause is a sustained workload power burst (30 W for 921 ms, starting at 1.526 s) that was still running when the thermal trip fired at 1.997 s. Firmware cut the clock only once (1200 to 1000 MHz) and that did not lower power. The cause is not confirmed: the sampled temperatures never rose, so the 119 °C trip may come from a local hotspot or a bad trip-sensor reading.
- **Confidence:** 50% &nbsp;(llm:claude-opus-5-5:multiagent)
- **Rationale:** Causal chain: 30 W, 921 ms power burst (1.526 s) -> firmware lowers clock one step, 1200 to 1000 MHz (1.684 s), no further step-downs, no fan ramp logged, current unchanged -> heat outruns cooling headroom, probably at a hotspot the sampling sensors don't cover -> trip reports 119 °C against a 105 °C threshold (1.997 s) -> emergency shutdown (2.010 s). Three specialists (THERMAL, PMU, FW) independently point to a workload burst, and the closest past incident (run 000020, similarity 0.93) was resolved as a workload burst. The timing fits: the burst started 471 ms before the trip and was still running when it hit. The signs of the other causes are missing. A poor heatsink would show a sensor-to-ambient delta warning and a slow, steady climb; neither appears. A bad fan profile would show fan speed below target RPM; that doesn't appear either. A regulator limit would show droop counts or current-limit events, but droop_cnt is 0 on every sample and there are no current-limit events. Boot shows no fallback profile being loaded.

I'm holding confidence at about 0.5 because of one big inconsistency. All four sensors stayed flat at 93-97 °C through the burst. The single 119 °C value appears only on the trip line, which names no sensor, and sensor 3 read 97 °C one millisecond later. Core-rail current also stayed flat at about 1.25 A and did not drop after the clock step-down. So the logs don't show the burst actually heating the die. A local hotspot not covered by the sampling sensors, or a false trip, are both still possible (that would be "OTHER"). I didn't send anyone back with a follow-up, because the existing logs can't settle this: the trip line names no sensor, there are no register dumps, and there is no fan telemetry. The answer needs new data. (3 of 4 specialists pointed at this cause.)

**Recommended actions:**
1. Re-run the same workload with per-sensor logging on the trip path, recording which sensor raised the trip, plus a raw ADC dump at the trip, to tell a real hotspot from a false reading.
1. Replay the 30 W, about 900 ms burst on a known-good board with good cooling: if it trips again at a similar time, it's a workload burst; if not, look at this unit's sensor or heatsink.
1. Turn on fan PWM/RPM and target-RPM logging, and firmware governor logging (thresholds, polling period, step size), to check whether the fan ramped and why firmware stopped after one step.
1. Capture package or domain power, not just the core rail, to check 30 W against this part's power budget and to see why the step-down didn't cut current.
1. If it is confirmed as a burst, consider stronger throttling: several clock step-downs or a burst power cap, plus a fan escalation tied to burst detection.
