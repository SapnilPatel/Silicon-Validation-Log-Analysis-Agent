### 000023:PMU_DROOP
- **Root cause:** The VCORE regulator hit its 2000 mA current limit (OCP) and folded back. That caused a fast droop from 850 to 611 mV and crossed the 750 mV brown-out threshold. The brown-out started a watchdog. Nothing cleared it, so it expired 3733 ms later and asserted SoC reset (reason=BROWNOUT_WDOG).
- **Confidence:** 78% &nbsp;(llm:claude-opus-5-5:multiagent)
- **Rationale:** Causal chain: VCORE current spike past the 2000 mA limit (4.432 s) -> regulator current-limit foldback -> fast droop 850->611 mV, cause=regulator_limit (4.448 s) -> brown-out threshold of 750 mV crossed (4.457 s) -> watchdog started by the brown-out, never cleared even though the rail recovered (~4.56 s) and firmware kept running -> watchdog timeout after wd_ms=3733 = 8190-4457 (8.190 s) -> SoC reset BROWNOUT_WDOG (8.192 s). The PMU log matches the RC_REGULATOR_LIMIT signature exactly: an explicit "Regulator current limit hit i_limit_ma=2000" line 16 ms before the droop, and the droop itself is tagged cause=regulator_limit. The other possible causes were each ruled out by a different specialist. There are no load_step or di/dt markers (rules out RC_LOAD_STEP_SPIKE). There is no ringing or undershoot, and the rail recovered cleanly within about 100 ms (rules out RC_INSUFFICIENT_DECOUPLING). Firmware shows no DVFS changes or workload bursts, and thermal shows a flat 53–57 °C with no power-burst line (rules out RC_WORKLOAD_BURST and the thermal causes). DDR shows no traffic burst and no damage after the event (rules out RC_DMA_BURST_OVERFLOW). The firmware specialist found that wd_ms equals the time since the brown-out exactly, and firmware heartbeats continued the whole time. So the reset was set off by the brown-out, not by a firmware hang. The historian's similar incidents are all THERMAL_TRIP or NOC runs and don't match. I'm keeping confidence below 0.85 for two reasons. First, the cause of the current going over 2 A wasn't captured, because telemetry samples too slowly to see it. Second, the 2 A limit hasn't been checked against the VCORE spec. A normal peak of about 1.4 A leaves only about 30% headroom, which suggests the limit is set too low or the regulator is undersized. (1 of 4 specialists pointed at this cause.)

1. Run a margin sweep: different workloads at reduced OCP limits to measure the actual headroom.
